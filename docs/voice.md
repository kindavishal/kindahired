# How kindahired talks

The site, the app, Telegram and the desktop app all speak in one voice. This page is the reference
for writing any of it.

## What we're saying

Your next job is probably already in your network: in your inbox, your LinkedIn messages, your X
DMs and your calendar. It slips away because that context is scattered, acting on it takes too
long, and follow-ups get forgotten. kindahired brings it together, does the research, and tells you
the next move on time, so you get in front of the hiring team fast. You still make every move.

The story comes before the mechanics. Say what changes for the person, not how the pipeline works.
How it works, where data goes and how to set it up belong on their own pages.

## Who we're talking to

People who are on LinkedIn and X, who use AI tools at work, and who are juggling more leads than
they can keep track of. They have the network and the tools. Nothing is making them work together
on their own search.

## Tone: an honest friend

- **Simple words, full sentences.** Write the way you'd say it out loud to a friend. "You had a
  great call, then the holidays happened" beats "Lead decay during low-engagement periods".
- **Honest, never hype.** Job hunting is hard and the app won't promise a job. No "supercharge",
  "10x", "unlock", "seamless", "effortless" or exclamation marks.
- **Warm, a little wry.** It's fine to admit things go wrong. That's the name: kinda hired.
- **"You" for the reader, "I" only in the founder story.** The bot may say "I" in Telegram
  ("I'll remind you").
- **Specific beats general.** Name the place (email, LinkedIn, an X DM), the person (the
  recruiter, the hiring manager), and the time (today, after the holidays).

## Words

| Say | Don't say |
|---|---|
| your next job, the role | opportunity pipeline, funnel |
| your network, people you know | contacts graph, relationships data |
| prepare your application | build a pack |
| the next move, what to do next | action item, task queue |
| check in, send a thank-you, send a last note | close warmly, stay in touch |
| could rule you out | hard gate |
| how you work | playbook |
| morning summary | digest |
| job pages to check | watched pages, scan |
| the app on your computer | worker, hub, Claude step |

## The rule for helper text

If a sentence has to explain how a screen works, change the screen instead: a clearer heading, a
button that says what it does, a label that names the thing. Keep text that carries information
(a date, a count, a reason) and promises about privacy and trust.

## Things we always keep true

- It never applies, sends a message or follows up for you. Every send is the person's.
- It never invents facts about the person.
- It's free: early access, or run it yourself from the public repo.
